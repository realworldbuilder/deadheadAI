Hail, netheads.

Where the name comes from, and why an app answers to it forty years later.

Before there was a web to surf, there was a Dead newsgroup on Usenet, and the people on it had a problem the band never had: they knew each other only as usernames. You could argue about the Bird Song from last night with someone for a year and walk right past them in the lot.

Spring 1982

A programmer at an early networking company was on the net and on the bus at the same time, and he did what programmers do when they need a label for something: he made up a word. Nethead. By his own account it was used electronically before it was ever said out loud. His opening post to the Dead newsgroup began, “Hail netheads.” It stuck.

The shirt

The word alone didn’t solve the problem. Screens were character-based, so nobody had a picture of anybody. What solved it was a shirt. His partner Maya drew the design from what she saw when she walked into the room while he was at the computer: he turned around and gave her a friendly wave. She sewed three by hand — one for him, one for David Gans, one for Marty Lefkowitz — and then a batch was made for the Frost shows, 10/9 and 10/10/82, so the net could find itself in the crowd at Stanford.

The back carried a line the netheads voted into existence themselves, and it went on the back for a practical reason: you dance facing the band, so the back is what the head behind you reads. Four decades on the shirt is still made, by the same people, and still doing the job.

On the DEC side

The word travelled the way tapes did. By 1991 the Deadheads inside Digital Equipment Corporation, who had their own notesfile on the company network and called themselves DECheads, were using it for the rec.music.gdead crowd they met on tour — at the Blarney Stone during the nine-night MSG run, up in the tapers’ section, on the semi-annual shirt offer somebody named road-runner ran on the newsgroup. Three notes from the archive, the way they looked on a terminal:

CSLALL::HENDERSON · GRATEFUL · September 1991, last night of the MSG run

…although I certainly enjoyed meeting all the NETheads.

CSCMA::M_PECKAR · GRATEFUL · 1993, atop a most-wanted list

…all of these tapes came to me from Netheads past and present.

NACAD2::SIEGEL · GRATEFUL 45.352 · 1994

Alright! A nethead becomes a DEChead! Welcome to the club!

That last one is the whole idea in ten words. The net was never the point. Finding each other was.

Why the app

The Internet Archive holds thousands of Dead tapes, and under every one of them is the same conversation the netheads were having in 1982 — which source to trust, where the second set catches fire, who was on the floor that night — typed by heads for heads who will never meet. Nethead reads all of it and rides shotgun. It is a head from the net, in your pocket, waving hello.

That is why our mark is a skeleton at an old computer. It has been at the terminal a long time. It just turned around to say hey now.
