Deadhead AI

The music never stopped. Neither should discovering it.

★★★★★

You know how it goes. Somebody says “man, you gotta hear 5/8/77” and suddenly it’s 3am, you’re six shows deep, and Jerry is peeling the paint off some field house in 1974. There are thousands of tapes on the Internet Archive — audience tapes, soundboards, matrixes, thirty years of the boys cooking — and the only map has ever been the heads in the comment section.

Deadhead AI puts one of those heads in your pocket. It’s an AI companion for the Archive’s Grateful Dead collection: ask it anything the way you’d ask the guy on the rail, and every answer points at a real tape with a real setlist. The app hosts no music. It just knows the road.

Ask like a head

“Mellow ’73 for a rainy Sunday.” “Hottest Scarlet>Fire of the eighties.” “I’ve got a five hour drive, build me a second set that never lands.” Plain English in, real recordings out. No hallucinated bootlegs, ever.

A guide for every show

Open any night and get the story: why this one matters, which source to spin, where the X-factor kicks in. Decades of listener reviews and ratings help pick the best tape of the night — the way it’s always been done, just faster.

— every show page, basically

Shelves that build themselves

Anniversaries, tour seasons, songs rising out of your own listening. The shelves refresh through the day like a friend who keeps taping things for you. Pin one and it’s yours to keep.

Journeys through the catalog

Primal ’68 weirdness. Europe ’72. The ’77 peak. The MIDI years (respect the MIDI years). Guided paths that walk you show by show with the context the liner notes never gave you.

Free AI, on the house

Sign in with Apple and the full AI brain is free — and your shelves and journal ride along in your own iCloud. Skip the sign-in and everything still works on a curated offline knowledge base. No ads, no trackers, nobody selling your trip diary.
